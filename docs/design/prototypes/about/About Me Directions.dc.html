About me

I started shooting weddings to pay for a camera, and stayed for the part where nobody is performing.

Most of my work is documentary and commercial: tailors, kitchens, courts, workshops. I shoot with one camera and available light where it serves the frame, and I grade my own material because the grade is where the intent either survives or does not.

Before this I cut other people's footage for three years, which is the best training there is for knowing what you actually need to shoot.

About

I light rooms that already have light in them, and I stay until the room gives something back.

Cinematographer and photographer in Hanoi. Most of the work is documentary and commercial — tailors, kitchens, courts, workshops — shot with one camera and the light the room already has.

I grade my own material. The grade is where the intent either survives or it does not, and handing that to someone else has never once gone the way I imagined it.

Before this I cut other people's footage for three years — the best training there is for knowing what you actually need to shoot, and how little of it that usually is.

The tailor's workroom, before the lights

I work small and close, usually alone, occasionally with a gaffer when the room is bigger than the light. If a project needs a crew of twelve, I am probably not the right call.
